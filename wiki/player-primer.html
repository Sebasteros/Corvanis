Player Primer: Start Here

This page is for players who are new to Corvanis or new to 5e. Read it first, then build your character using Character Creation. You do not need to memorize anything. Every number on your sheet has a short explanation below.

The world in one paragraph

Corvanis is a city-state where almost everything runs on credits, debt, and access. Vantara, its great city, is stacked into four levels: the Apex at the top, the Spires below it, the Grid where most people work and live, and the SubZ at the bottom, where the city's old and forgotten things end up. Your Access ID decides which levels you can enter. Your debt decides how much the city wants from you. Your augments (cybernetic parts) make you stronger, but each one costs you Load, and too much Load starts to break a body down.

How a roll works

Corvanis uses the same dice as 5e. Roll a d20, add your modifier, add your proficiency bonus if you are proficient in the skill or save, and try to meet or beat the Difficulty Class (DC) the GM gives. Advantage and disadvantage work exactly as in 5e.

Your character sheet, one number at a time

Number What it is How to get it
Ability scores Your six 5e scores: Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom, Charisma. Assign the standard array (15, 14, 13, 12, 10, 8) or use point buy, then add your Origin bonuses.
Modifier The bonus from an ability score: (score minus 10), rounded down, then divided by 2. A 14 gives +2. A 9 gives -1.
Proficiency bonus +2 at level 2. Fixed.
Hit points How much damage you can take before you fall. Your Role's hit die, plus your Constitution modifier, at each level. The Character Creation page shows the steps.
Load limit How much cybernetic weight your body can carry. Equal to your Constitution score, plus any bonus from your Origin or feats.
Surge The energy you spend on tech abilities, Intrusions, and some augments. 2 plus your Role's Surge modifier, with a minimum of 1. See Surge.
Credits Money. You start with 1,000 cr. Fixed at the start.
Debt What you owe. Your GM sets it. A suggested start is 5,000 cr.

Load: what your augments cost

Each augment you own has a Load cost. Add them up. That total is your Total Load, and it cannot be higher than your Load limit unless you wear a redline rig. A redline rig lets you carry more. Each point you go over costs you hit points, and you roll for the cost before you commit, so you can see the risk first. The full rule is on the Augments page. Your starting augment from your Role has its own Load cost, so note it. You do not lose anything by having low Load. Higher-tier augments cost more Load, and more Load means a stronger body but less room for the rest.

Augments and the skill tree

Augments are organized into five lines: Reflex (limbs and speed), Optic (eyes), Dermal (skin and defense), Frame (bone and strength), and Neural (brain and rig). Each line has three tiers, with three nodes in each tier. The first tier is easy to reach. The second and third tiers need a node from the tier below, in the same line.

  • Your Role gives you one free starting node.
  • Your GM awards skill points. Each point lets you install one node you qualify for. You still pay its Load.
  • You can also buy a node at a clinic for its credit price, if you have the money.

The full branching tree is on the Augments and Skill Trees page.

Credits, debt, and access

  • Credits pay for gear, services, and augments.
  • Debt grows when you miss payments. Your standing can go from Clear to Overdue to Flagged, and at the worst end the lender can issue a repossession notice. Your GM decides what that means for your character.
  • Access IDs decide where you can go. Every character has a Civic ID for public areas. A Shift Pass opens the Grid. A Chrome ID is rare and opens the Spires and the Apex. See The World.

Relics

A relic is a unique tool with its own power and charges. Relics ask you to make a save when they are used, and the DC is set on the relic's page. See Relics if your character carries one.

Finishing your character

Work through these in order. Each one is explained in Character Creation.

  1. Choose an Origin (what you are, and your ability bonuses).
  2. Choose a Role (your class: Edgerunner, Infiltrator, Netrunner, Fixer, Techie, Enforcer, or Medtech).
  3. Assign your six ability scores.
  4. Note your hit points, Load limit, and Surge.
  5. Choose a background (two skills, a tool, and who you know).
  6. Note your Access ID, passes, credits, and debt.
  7. Note your starting augment and its Load cost.
  8. Buy gear with your credits.
  9. Write your backstory (see below).

What to send your GM: the backstory

Send the following. It can be short, but the more detail you give, the more your GM can weave your story into the first session.

  • Name: what your character is called on the street, and any other name they use.
  • Before Vantara: where you came from, and what your life was like before the city.
  • What you owe: your debt, to whom, and why.
  • Who you do not trust: one person, faction, or thing you keep your guard up against.
  • Your goal: what you want most in the city right now.

Three lines are enough to start. A full paragraph for each point is better.

Questions

If something on your sheet does not make sense, ask your GM before the session. It is far easier to fix a number on the table before you play than in the middle of a fight.
