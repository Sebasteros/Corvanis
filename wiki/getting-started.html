Getting Started

What you need

  • The 5e Player's Handbook rules (or the free System Reference Document), for dice, ability checks, combat, and rests.
  • This bible, open to the pages you use most.
  • A character sheet and a pencil. Dice: d4, d6, d8, d10, d12, d20, d100.
  • A group of three to five players and one Game Master (GM, also called the DM).
  • Session time of about two to three hours, played in person or online.

How a session works

Corvanis plays the way any 5e game does: the GM describes a scene, players say what they do, and the dice decide what happens. The differences are:

  • Scenes, not clock times. Sessions move from scene to scene. The GM can say "later that night" without tracking minutes.
  • Cycles. Time between sessions passes in cycles. Each cycle, characters pay debts, collect pay, and get news. The GM runs each cycle.
  • Access. Each area of the city asks for a pass or ID. Find out what your character can enter before the session starts.

The rules you change first

Five parts of 5e are replaced or adjusted in Corvanis. Learn these before you read anything else:

  1. Load replaces Humanity. Augments cost Load. Your body can carry only so much. See Augments.
  2. Surge replaces spell slots and class resources. Netrunners, augmented fighters, and medics spend Surge. See Surge.
  3. Credits and debt. Money is credits. Many characters start in debt. See Character Creation.
  4. Access IDs. Civic ID, Shift Pass, and Chrome ID decide where you can go. See The World.
  5. Relics. Unique tools with their own saves and charges. See Relics.

Everything else works as written in 5e. If a rule is not in this bible, use the 5e rule.

A first character in ten minutes

  1. Pick an Origin and a Role.
  2. Assign ability scores, then apply your Origin bonuses.
  3. Pick a background from Character Creation.
  4. Note your Role's starting augment and its Load cost.
  5. Check your Access ID and debt.
  6. Write down your Surge, Load limit, and starting credits.

Then bring the sheet to the table. The GM will weave your backstory into the first session.

Safety and tone

Corvanis deals with debt, medical neglect, forced repossession, and loss of identity. Talk with your group before play about what is off-limits, and agree on a way to pause a scene (a word or a hand signal). The GM can soften any scene without asking first.
