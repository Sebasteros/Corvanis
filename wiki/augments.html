Augments and Skill Trees

Augments are the cybernetic parts that make Corvanis characters more than human: limbs, eyes, skin, bone, and rig ports. In place of a Humanity score, Corvanis uses Load. Every augment costs Load. Your body can only carry so much before it starts to fail.

Load

  • Load limit: Equal to your Constitution score (plus any bonuses from your Origin or feats).
  • Load cost: Each augment has a Load cost, shown on its node.
  • Total Load: The sum of the Load costs of all augments you own.
  • The cap: Your total Load cannot be higher than your Load limit, unless you wear a redline rig (below).
  • Lowering Load: Some clinics can remove augments. Removing an augment refunds its Load and costs half its price.

Redline: going past your limit

A redline rig is a regulator that lets a body carry more hardware than it should. It keeps the augments running by taking the difference out of you.

  • The cost: Before you pay for a node that takes you past your limit, roll one die for every point of Load over. The die is a d3 at levels 1 to 4, a d6 at levels 5 to 8, and a d8 at levels 9 and 10. Add the dice together. That total is how many hit points your maximum drops by. You see the roll before you commit, so you can take the risk or walk away. The number is final once you pay.
  • How far: Each rig lets you go a set number of points over your limit. You can wear one rig at a time. A rig is not an augment and costs no Load.
  • The floor: You cannot redline so far that your hit point maximum would fall below half of its normal value.
  • Healing: While you are redlined, nothing can heal you above the lowered maximum.
  • Backing off: If you remove augments and drop back under your limit, your hit point maximum returns after a long rest.
  • Without a rig: You cannot go over your limit. In an emergency the GM may allow it for one scene. If so, the GM rolls on the Glitch table once for each point over.
Rig Price Load past the limit Where Notes
Redline Shunt 800 cr 2 Any clinic A belt unit with two lines into the spine.
Redline Harness 2,400 cr 4 Vitalis or Helix clinic Needs character level 5.
Red Valve 600 cr 6 The Stitchery Black Market. Each time you drop to 0 hit points, the GM rolls on the Glitch table.

Example: Rin has a Constitution of 13, so her Load limit is 13. She is level 3 and carries 15 Load with a Redline Shunt. She is 2 over, so she rolls two d4 before she pays. She rolls a 3 and a 4, so her hit point maximum drops by 7. She can still take the node, or back out and keep her current Load.

Installation takes one cycle at a clinic. A Medtech with Cutting Edge can install a Common augment in a scene.

How the skill tree works

Augments are organized into five lines. Each line has three tiers, and each tier has three nodes. Owning a node gives you its effect. A higher tier costs more Load and more credits, and it does more: a Common node is a small edge, a Mil-spec node changes how you fight or work, and a Prime node is something a character is known for.

  • Tier I: Any node in a line can be taken.
  • Tier II: You must own one Tier I node in the same line.
  • Tier III: You must own one Tier II node in the same line.
  • Starting node: Your Role gives you one Tier I node for free (listed in your Role).
  • Growth: The GM awards skill points. One skill point installs one node you qualify for, and you still pay its Load. A good pace is one point at each level beyond level 2, plus a point for a major story beat. You can also buy a node at a clinic for its credit price instead of spending a point.
  • Cross-line: You may take a node from a second line only if you own a Tier I node there.
  • Surge costs: Some node effects cost Surge. The Surge pool is covered in Surge.

Synthetic chassis

Synthetics (see Origins) may take any augment line, but each augment costs 1 less Load (to a minimum of 1). Synthetics cannot use Vitalis Cover and cannot install Black Market augments without a Medtech.

Cyberware by body system

This section is a second catalog of original augments, one set per body system. Every augment follows the same rules:

  • One effect each. An augment gives one bonus, one AC bonus, or one once-per-rest power. Nothing gives more than +2 to a score or AC.
  • Level gates. A Common augment (Tier I) can be taken at any level. A Mil-spec augment (Tier II) needs character level 5. A Prime augment (Tier III) needs character level 9.
  • Limit on powers. A character may have at most two augments with a once-per-rest power in each body system. This keeps combat varied.
  • Load and price follow the tier table at the top of this page.
