Vitalis Rescue

Vitalis Rescue is the city's private emergency medicine company. It sells coverage by the cycle. The tiers below are based on what each covered client gets when they are hurt.

Tier Price What it covers
Walk-in 200 cr per treatment Stabilization and basic repairs. No subscription.
Grid Cover 50 cr per cycle Response within the scene in the Grid. Stabilization at 0 hit points, once per cycle. Common augment installs.
Spire Cover 200 cr per cycle Priority response across the Spires. Mil-spec installs at 25 percent off.
Apex Cover 800 cr per cycle Full coverage on the Tether route and in Apex. Prime installs, emergency transport, and any augment repair.

Vitalis logs every treatment to the client's Registrar file. A covered character who is treated gets a note in that file (see Factions). The GM decides how the file is used.

Pierce The shot ignores half and three-quarters cover, and passes through thin walls and doors.

Linked weapons

A linked weapon has a small rig of its own, and it answers to a mind. You need the Port Jack augment to use its link.

  • Linking: Use a bonus action to link to a weapon you are holding. You stay linked until you let go of it, fall unconscious, or link to something else.
  • While linked: You gain +1 to attack rolls with it, nobody else can fire it, and you can use its Link effect.
  • One at a time: You can be linked to one weapon. With the Rig Link node you can be linked to two.
  • Unlinked: Without a Port Jack, or without linking, it is an ordinary weapon with the listed damage and no Link effect.
  • Cut off: The Lockout Intrusion, or a Pulse Canister, breaks your link until the end of your next turn.
  • Stolen weapons: A linked weapon remembers its owner. Binding it to a new owner takes a Techkit or a deck, 10 minutes, and a DC 15 Intelligence check.

Vehicles

A vehicle acts on its driver's turn. Driving is part of your movement. Anything risky takes a Dexterity check, and you add your proficiency bonus if you have the Dock Hand background, the Techie Role, or the GM agrees your character has driven for a living.

  • Speed is how far the vehicle moves on a turn in a fight or a chase. On an open road, a vehicle covers about 1 mile per minute for every 100 feet of speed.
  • AC and hit points work as they do for a creature. A vehicle at 0 hit points stops, and everyone inside takes 2d6 bludgeoning damage if it was moving.
  • Hardness: A vehicle ignores any single hit that deals less damage than its Hardness.
  • Ramming: A vehicle deals 1d6 bludgeoning damage for every 20 feet of its speed to what it hits, and takes half of that itself. A creature can make a DEX save (DC 13) to dive clear and take half.
  • Cover: Passengers have half cover. In an armored vehicle with the windows up, they have three-quarters cover.
